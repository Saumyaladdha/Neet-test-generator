## MATCH THE COLUMN - MEDIUM LEVEL (CHEMISTRY)

**Question Format:** Intermediate matching with 4-5 pairs

**How to Identify MEDIUM Match the Column:**
- Matching requires understanding SPECIFIC properties or REACTIONS
- Some properties may SEEM to apply to multiple compounds (but don't)
- Column II contains more TECHNICAL terms or reaction products
- Student must know specific details, not just general properties
- May include reaction products or specific applications

**Rules:**
- Use 4-5 pairs
- Pairs from image content
- Some pairs may require combining information
- All elements must be from the image

**TABLE FORMAT (MANDATORY - USE LaTeX):**
Use LaTeX tabular format for tables, with EXACTLY 4 items per column:
\begin{{tabular}}{{|l|l|}}
\hline
Column I & Column II \\
\hline
1. [Item] & i. [Match] \\
2. [Item] & ii. [Match] \\
3. [Item] & iii. [Match] \\
4. [Item] & iv. [Match] \\
\hline
\end{{tabular}}

**Options Format (roman numerals ONLY — never letters for Column II references):**
a) 1-iii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-ii
b) 1-ii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-iii
c) 1-iii, 2-iv, 3-i, 4-ii
d) 1-iv, 2-iii, 3-ii, 4-i

**IMPORTANT - SHUFFLE COLUMN II (MANDATORY):**
- Column II items MUST be shuffled/randomized so correct matches are NON-SEQUENTIAL
- NEVER arrange Column II so that correct answer is 1-i, 2-ii, 3-iii, 4-iv (sequential)
- The correct matching should be scrambled like: 1-iii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-ii
- This ensures students must actually know the content, not just match by position

**Example of Proper Shuffling:**
Wrong setup: 1-i, 2-ii, 3-iii, 4-iv (too easy - sequential match)
Correct setup: 1-iii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-ii (shuffled - requires knowledge)

**Complexity:**
- Include related but distinct concepts as distractors
- Shuffled options should be plausible at first glance

**MANDATORY — OPTION GENERATION (PREVENTS "NO CORRECT ANSWER" BUG):**
1. After building the table, write the correct matching internally: CORRECT = 1-[x], 2-[y], 3-[z], 4-[w]
2. Copy CORRECT into one option slot exactly, character-by-character.
3. Build the other 3 options by swapping 2+ pairings from CORRECT — never generate all 4 options independently.
4. Before finalizing, verify: exactly ONE option matches CORRECT exactly; the other 3 do not. If zero or more than one match, fix before outputting.
5. Copy the CORRECT line verbatim into the output's `correct_sequence` field (see schema) — an internal quality-check field, stripped before delivery, never shown to students.

**Example from Source Text:**
Source 1: "Sodium reacts vigorously with water producing hydrogen gas"
Source 2: "Calcium reacts slowly with cold water"
Source 3: "Iron reacts with steam to form iron oxide"
Source 4: "Gold does not react with water"
↓
\begin{{tabular}}{{|l|l|}}
\hline
Column I & Column II \\
\hline
1. Sodium & i. No reaction with water \\
2. Calcium & ii. Reacts with steam \\
3. Iron & iii. Vigorous reaction with cold water \\
4. Gold & iv. Slow reaction with cold water \\
\hline
\end{{tabular}}

a) 1-iii, 2-iv, 3-ii, 4-i
b) 1-i, 2-ii, 3-iii, 4-iv
c) 1-ii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-iii
d) 1-iv, 2-iii, 3-i, 4-ii

Answer: a (1-iii, 2-iv, 3-ii, 4-i)

**Why this is MEDIUM:** All metals are mentioned but with different reactivity. Student must remember specific reaction conditions for each metal.
