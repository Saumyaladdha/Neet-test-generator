## TEXT FORMATTING RULES (MANDATORY - USE LATEX)

You MUST use LaTeX syntax for all chemical formulas, equations, and scientific notation:

1. NO MARKDOWN FORMATTING:
   - DO NOT use ** for bold
   - DO NOT use * for italics
   - Write text normally, use LaTeX only for scientific notation

2. CHEMICAL FORMULAS - Use subscripts and superscripts:
   - $H_2O$ (water)
   - $CO_2$ (carbon dioxide)
   - $H_2SO_4$ (sulphuric acid)
   - $HNO_3$ (nitric acid)
   - $NaOH$ (sodium hydroxide)
   - $NH_3$ (ammonia)
   - $CH_4$ (methane)
   - $C_2H_5OH$ (ethanol)
   - $C_6H_{12}O_6$ (glucose)
   - $Na^+$, $Cl^-$, $Ca^{2+}$, $Fe^{3+}$ (ions)
   - $SO_4^{2-}$, $PO_4^{3-}$, $OH^-$ (polyatomic ions)

3. CHEMICAL EQUATIONS - Use reaction arrows:
   - $\rightarrow$ (irreversible reaction)
   - $\rightleftharpoons$ (reversible/equilibrium)
   - $2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$ (combustion)
   - $N_2 + 3H_2 \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3$ (Haber process)
   - $\xrightarrow{\Delta}$ (heated)
   - $\xrightarrow{hv}$ (light-driven)
   - $\xrightarrow{\text{catalyst}}$ (catalysed)

4. STATE SYMBOLS (use \text inside math):
   - $(g)$, $(l)$, $(s)$, $(aq)$ — e.g. $NaCl\,(aq)$

5. SUPERSCRIPTS for oxidation states and charges:
   - $Fe^{2+}$, $Fe^{3+}$, $Cu^{2+}$, $Zn^{2+}$
   - $\overset{+6}{S}$ (oxidation state notation if needed)

6. GREEK LETTERS - Use LaTeX Greek commands:
   - $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ particles
   - $\Delta H$ (enthalpy change)
   - $\Delta G$ (Gibbs free energy)
   - $\Delta S$ (entropy change)
   - $\lambda$ (wavelength)

7. MATH SYMBOLS:
   - $\approx$ (approximately)
   - $\pm$ (plus-minus)
   - $\times$ (multiplication)
   - $\degree C$ (degree Celsius)
   - $\mu$ (micro)
   - $10^{-3}$, $10^{23}$ (powers of ten)
   - $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ (Avogadro's number)

8. QUANTITIES AND UNITS:
   - $K_a$, $K_b$, $K_c$, $K_p$ (equilibrium constants)
   - $K_w$ (ionic product of water)
   - $pH = -\log[H^+]$
   - $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ or $M$ (molarity)
   - $\text{g mol}^{-1}$ (molar mass)
